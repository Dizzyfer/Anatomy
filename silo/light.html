Grain dust explosion

Tap: hot bearing

Grain dust burns like a gas once it's in the air. The first bang is usually small and inside equipment: a hot bearing or a slipping belt lights the dust cloud inside a bucket elevator. That primary is not what kills people. Its pressure wave shakes everything in the building and throws the dust that's settled on beams, ledges and floors into the air, and the primary's flame lights that. The secondary runs through the whole building. A layer about 0.8 mm thick, roughly a paperclip wire, over more than 5 % of the floor is enough for NFPA 654 to call it an explosion hazard. Monitoring stops the ignition, vents take the punch out of the primary, isolation keeps it out of the silos, and housekeeping takes away the fuel for the big one.

A toy, not a simulator. Real: primary and secondary dust explosions, NFPA 654's 0.8 mm (1/32 in) over 5 % of the floor (for dust around 1200 kg/m³), OSHA's 3.2 mm (1/8 in) for priority areas in grain handling, and bearing monitoring, venting and isolation as protections.
