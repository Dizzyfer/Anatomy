IP ratings

Tap: run the next test

An IP code is two numbers, each earned in its own test. The first is solids, up to 6 for dust-tight: the enclosure sits in talcum powder with a vacuum pulling on the inside. The second is water, and it is not one ladder. Up to 6, each number covers the ones below it. But 7 and 8 (immersion) and 9 (hot high-pressure jets) are separate tests, so a box that sat a metre under water for half an hour has not been shown to stand up to a jet. That is why you see double markings like IP66/IP67. Every test finds a different path in. A jet gets under a lightly clamped lid that a metre of still water presses shut, and a drain hole that a jet never reaches floods the moment the box goes under.

A toy, not a simulator. Real: the IEC 60529 test conditions for IPX5, IPX6, IPX7 and IPX9 (nozzle sizes, flows, distances, depth and times), dust testing in talcum powder under a vacuum, and the rule that 7, 8 and 9 do not cover the jet numbers, so both get marked. Made up: how much gets in through each fault, the pass marks, and the scale: the nozzle and the water depth are drawn far closer than they really are. Real tests are not done live, either.
