Hazardous areas

Ex d flamepath

Pick a gas, set the flange gap, hit fire. Under MESG the box holds. Over it, the bench goes up.
Hazardous areas

Pressure piling

Two flameproof compartments and an opening between them. The first explosion pumps up the second, the flame follows, and the small one can go off at three times the pressure a single box makes.
Hazardous areas

Ex i: cable length

Pay out cable, hit spark. Every metre stores more energy. Past the gas's MIE, Zone 1 goes up.
Hazardous areas

Ex e: MDP and creepage

Load up the terminals until the box cooks past T4, or crank the voltage on a grubby surface until it tracks and arcs.
Hazardous areas

Ex m: encapsulation

The relay sparks away inside the resin and nothing happens. Thin the pour, cook the parts or pull the fuse, and the gas gets in.
Hazardous areas

Ex t: dust and housekeeping

A light buried until it smoulders, and a box with a missing plug filling with conductive dust until it shorts. Primary, then the secondary.
Hazardous areas

Ex p: purge and pressure

Purge five volumes, hold 50 Pa, keep the door shut. Or put the intake in the zone and fool the interlock completely.
Lighting

How an LED works

Current makes light, voltage barely moves, most of it is heat. Run it off constant voltage on a weak heatsink and watch it run away.
Electrical safety

Arc flash

Fault level, clearing time, distance, PPE. Drop the spanner. Less fault current can mean way more energy.
Electrical

How a motor starts

Direct on line, star-delta or VFD, on a fan, a loaded conveyor or a jammed rotor. Watch the inrush, then bypass the overload.
Electrical

IP ratings

Build a box, then put it through the dust chamber, the hoses and the dunk tank. A drain hole passes the jet and floods in the tank. Hand-tight screws do the opposite.
Batteries

Lithium thermal runaway

Overcharge, nail or cook one cell. Chemistry, charge level and spacing decide whether it takes the whole pack.
Lighting

Lost neutral

Break one circuit's neutral and the row goes off, with a live neutral. Break the shared link and nothing goes off: the star point floats and the drivers cook.
Lighting

Stroboscopic effect

A lathe under a flickering light. At 2,000 rpm under 100 Hz the chuck looks parked, so the dummy reaches in. Change the driver or the PWM rate and the blur comes back.
Just for fun

How a lock gets picked

Shear line, binding order, set pins, oversets and spool false sets. Drag the pins up yourself and pick it.
Just for fun

Espresso machine

Grind, dose, pressure and tamp. Dial in a shot, choke it, gush it or channel it. Then jam the safety valve and watch the boiler leave.
Hazardous areas

Hazardous area zones

Grade of release, dilution and availability through the zone table. Shrink the ventilation and watch Zone 2 turn into Zone 1.
Things that blow up

Hot water system

Stuck thermostat, welded cutout, capped relief valve. Water at 224 °C that can't boil, until the tank lets go.
Things that blow up

Grain dust explosion

A hot bearing lights the elevator leg. Then the dust on the beams does the real damage. Housekeeping, vents, isolation.
Things that blow up

Gas leak in the kitchen

Natural gas fills it from the ceiling, LPG from the floor. Too lean, flammable, too rich. Then somebody flicks the light.
Things that break

Truss bridge

Drive cars, road trains and a 620 t haul truck over a steel truss. Red pulls, blue pushes. Cut a member and it's in the river.
Things that blow up

Overclocking a CPU

Clock up, voltage up, blue screen, repeat. Tower, AIO or LN2 pot. Turn the safeties off and the VRM lets go.
Things that blow up

Jet engine

The biggest airliner engine, cut in half. Throttle to the 597 kN record, then feed it a goose, a flock or a fan blade.
Things that break

Hydro dam

Make a gigawatt, trip the grid, bring a flood. Jam the spillway, block the drains, and the town's in trouble.
Things that blow up

Nuclear reactor

Chernobyl's RBMK or a modern PWR on a toggle. Same abuse, very different endings. Run the 1986 test yourself.
Things that break

Aeroplane wing

Real airflow round a slice of it. Fly it slow and stall it, pull 4 g and snap it, or dive past the flutter speed.
Things that blow up

Power transformer

Cook the paper in a heatwave, kill the fans, hit it with lightning. Buchholz and differential save it, unless you turn them off.
Things that break

Car crash

A 1959 car and a 2009 car into the same barrier. Crumple zone, cage, belt and airbag, and how many g the dummy takes.
Things that break

F1 car

Downforce, drag and 2026 active aero. Sit in dirty air, cook the brakes past 1,000 °C, or lose the rear wing at 340 km/h.
